# Production-blocking fixes: explore filters, feed freshness, tab crash, share page (v2)

This is a second review pass after fixes were applied for the feed overflow (#1), broken hasNextPage (#2), and recent posts bypassing scoring (#4) from the first review. The recommendation engine now caps `recentBoostPosts` to the page limit, applies `scorePost` to all three pools, and determines `hasNextPage` using the actual slot sizes plus a `candidatePosts.length >= totalFetchLimit` heuristic. The combined array is also explicitly sliced to `limit` as a safety net.

**Watch for:** On a high-volume day where all `totalFetchLimit` candidates are recent (< 24h), the preference and discovery pools are empty, `remainingSlots` is 0, and `hasNextPage` fires only on the `candidatePosts.length >= totalFetchLimit` condition — which is correct but means pagination degrades to a simple "did we fill the fetch buffer" check with no preference/discovery diversity on subsequent pages (likely). The artworkType case mismatch (#5 from prior review) remains unaddressed but is acknowledged as out-of-scope.

**Verdict**: APPROVED

## High-level view

The three prior blocking concerns are resolved. The `recentBoostPosts` pool is sliced to `limit` before calculating remaining slots, so `combined` can never exceed the page size. The `hasNextPage` logic uses actual slot sizes and adds `candidatePosts.length >= totalFetchLimit` as an upper-bound signal. Recent posts are scored by `scorePost` and sorted by popularity, preserving ranking quality within the boost pool.

## Recommendation engine pagination after the fix

The revised `hasNextPage` logic:

```js
const hasNextPage = candidatePosts.length >= totalFetchLimit ||
  preferencePosts.length > remainingPreferenceSlots ||
  discoveryPosts.length > remainingDiscoverySlots ||
  recentBoostPosts.length > limit;
```

The `candidatePosts.length >= totalFetchLimit` condition is the critical addition — without it, on a day where recent posts dominate, the pool-based conditions are all false (empty pools can't overflow their slot counts) and pagination silently stops.

The `recentBoostPosts.length > limit` condition covers the scenario where the fetch buffer wasn't full but recent posts alone exceeded one page — redundant with the buffer check in most cases but provides a tighter signal for that specific edge.

The remaining behavioral gap: when all candidates fall within the 24-hour window, every page is served exclusively from `recentBoostPosts`. The preference/discovery split becomes inert until the user scrolls far enough back that posts age past the 24-hour threshold. For a community that publishes fewer than `totalFetchLimit` posts per day this is invisible; for a high-volume community it means the feed loses personalization on the first several pages.
